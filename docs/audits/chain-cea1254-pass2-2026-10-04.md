# Torus chain audit, second pass

Audited on 2026-10-04 at `cea1254e34625e6b09c58f794de8793b5c12713c`, branch `merge/item6-sync2`. This supplements the [first audit](chain-cea1254-2026-10-04.md); its F01–F07 remain separate findings. No pull or branch change was performed for this pass.

**Four additional source-supported findings:** unchecked consensus heights, incomplete governance snapshots, duplicate consensus keys, and a validator replacement cap that admits no replacements for full sets of four or five. Findings remain candidates for failing production-code regression tests, following this directory's audit convention.

They model selected predicates and arithmetic; they do not execute Rust, verify signatures, form real certificates, or reproduce a network exploit. `cargo` and `rustc` remain unavailable on PATH. No production files were changed, no live chain was contacted, and nothing was committed or pushed.

## F08 Unchecked consensus heights can strand the execution feed

**Evidence.** There are two heights: the outer HotStuff `Block.height`, and the Torus header height inside its datum. Honest construction starts outer height at zero and advances it from its parent's outer height (`crates/hotstuff_rs/src/hotstuff/implementation.rs:588`), while the first Torus child is height one. That convention is not an admission rule.

`Block::is_correct` in `crates/hotstuff_rs/src/types/block.rs:86` checks the hash and justify certificate; hashing an attacker-selected height does not validate its progression. `safe_block` in `crates/hotstuff_rs/src/block_tree/invariants.rs:354` checks only the justify's safety and phase. The header/body binding at `implementation.rs:2826` checks that the two copies of the outer height agree, not that either follows the parent. `TorusApp::check_proposal_data` at `crates/torus-consensus/src/app.rs:5062` and `validate_block` at line 5320 call `check_parent_link` at line 1452, which validates the inner Torus height and parent hash only.

The commit guard in `crates/hotstuff_rs/src/block_tree/accessors/internal.rs:735` tests whether the lowest uncommitted block's justify names the committed frontier. It does not test consecutive heights within that chain. The commit loop indexes each block at its supplied height and advances the highest committed block. `crates/hotstuff_rs/src/committed_feed.rs:116` then walks every numeric height, pausing on the first missing entry once its frontier is anchored.

**Counterexample and impact.** Let committed parent P have outer height 7 and inner height 8. A malicious proposer supplies child C with the correct parent hashes and inner height 9, but outer height 9 instead of 8, recomputing the block hash. The reviewed validation predicates accept both its ancestry and application data. Subsequent honest proposals can extend C, allowing it to become committed. The commit walk sees contiguous parent hashes and indexes C at 9. An execution feed already at outer 7 stops at absent outer 8 and cannot deliver C or later descendants. Sync cannot fetch a canonical block at 8 because this certified parent chain omitted it. The model demonstrates the indexing/feed consequence; actual quorum formation and recovery need a multi-validator regression.

**Recommended fix.** Enforce outer genesis height zero and outer child height exactly parent height plus one, using checked arithmetic. Apply this before voting and on every insertion path, including sync and body recovery. Make a missing parent retryable rather than accepting an unchecked height. Also enforce the intended outer/inner height relationship in the Torus adapter and add a defensive numeric continuity check before advancing the commit index.

**Regression test.** Keep a valid Torus datum and parent certificate; vary only the outer height and recompute hashes. Test skipped, repeated, decreasing, and maximum heights through full proposals, header/body voting, and sync. Honest validators must reject them before voting/insertion. A valid consecutive chain must still feed every committed block across restart.

## F09 Missing snapshot rows admit new voting stake

**Evidence.** `crates/torus-economics/src/governance.rs:774–782` records the proposal's snapshot block and calls `snapshot_voter_weights`. That method at line 1332 enumerates current delegation/permanent-stake owners and writes rows only for positive weights. An address with no stake at creation therefore has no snapshot row.

`compute_vote_weight_at` at line 1306 returns the stored weight when present, but otherwise calls `compute_vote_weight` against current state. It does not first establish that the proposal predates the snapshot feature. `cast_vote` at line 823 uses this result. The compatibility fallback consequently applies to new proposals and new voters as well as legacy proposals.

**Counterexample and impact.** Alice has 100 units at proposal creation; Bob has none. After creation Alice reduces her active stake and Bob delegates 200 units. Alice still receives her stored weight of 100, while Bob receives a live weight of 200 instead of his snapshot weight of zero. Bob can influence an already-open vote with stake obtained after its declared snapshot. This breaks the snapshot defense and can alter majority and quorum outcomes. It requires real staking balance and a valid vote; this finding does not claim free voting weight or an instantaneous flash-loan attack.

A related incomplete snapshot exists at finalization: lines 930–932 derive quorum from **current** total staked supply and parameters. Even if missing voter rows are fixed, the threshold can move during a proposal. This should be addressed in the snapshot policy and its tests; it is not counted as a fifth finding here.

**Recommended fix.** Persist an explicit proposal-level snapshot version/completion marker. For a snapshotted proposal, a missing voter row must mean zero weight. If legacy fallback is required, select it using explicit proposal metadata or a migration boundary. Store the intended quorum basis and voting parameters with the snapshot so they cannot change underneath an open vote.

**Regression test.** Submit a proposal with an initially unstaked address, then delegate and attempt to vote from that address. Expect zero weight/rejection. Cover an existing zero-balance delegation row, new permanent stake, abstention, and an existing positive voter whose stake changes. Test legacy proposals separately and define whether post-snapshot supply changes may affect quorum.

## F10 Duplicate keys overwrite consensus voting power

**Evidence.** `register_validator` in `crates/torus-economics/src/staking.rs:43` checks commission, minimum self stake, existing registration by sender address, and oracle-signer conflicts. It does not check whether another validator owns the submitted consensus public key. The externally reachable handler at `crates/torus-bridge/src/native_executor.rs:7565` requires a governance whitelist entry, so this is not open registration by any funded account.

`submit_key_rotation` in `staking.rs:1090` checks current validators' installed keys at line 1115, but does not reserve keys targeted by other pending rotations. Two validators can request the same currently unused key before either request takes effect.

`compute_new_validator_set` in `crates/torus-economics/src/epoch.rs:35` ranks separate address records by stake. `plan_rotation` in `crates/torus-economics/src/epoch_plan.rs:154` substitutes pending keys without checking uniqueness. The plan therefore can contain two addresses with the same key. Conversion in `crates/torus-consensus/src/app.rs:4607–4612` inserts those keys into `ValidatorSetUpdates`. Its `insert` at `crates/hotstuff_rs/src/types/update_sets.rs:55` is a `HashMap` replacement: a later duplicate overwrites the earlier power; powers are not combined and the plan is not rejected. The plan's descending power order lets a smaller duplicate overwrite a larger validator's weight.

**Counterexample and impact.** Six installed validators have powers A=60, B=C=D=E=10, and F=30. An approved newcomer G stakes 10 and copies A's key, with the membership limit permitting seven. There is one arrival, so the cap for the six-member old set permits it. Staking/plan totals become 140 across seven addresses. After conversion, A's key has power 10 and the actual consensus total is 80 across six keys. If F and G share an adversary, their staked share is 40/140, below one third, but the adversary's independently controlled key F now has 30/80 of actual consensus power, above one third. The other keys have only 50, below the resulting quorum of 54. Withholding F's vote can therefore block quorum under the modeled installed set. G does not need A's private key to reduce A's weight through the collision.

This is a source-derived weighted-set counterexample, not an observed transition or takeover. Governance approval of G and the stated stake distribution are substantive prerequisites. Pending-key collisions provide a second way for the plan and installed set to disagree. Separately, key parsing currently skips invalid encodings at conversion; admission should reject such inputs explicitly too.

**Recommended fix.** Enforce unique, valid consensus keys across installed registrations and pending reservations. Make registration, scheduling, cancellation, and application update that ownership index consistently. Recheck the final plan before publishing it: no duplicate or invalid key, and the converted membership and powers must match exactly. Reject a malformed plan instead of silently collapsing entries.

**Regression test.** Attempt registration with an installed key and with a reserved pending key. Schedule two rotations to one unused key; the second must fail. Exercise a rotation/registration race in one block. Convert a deliberately malformed plan and require an explicit error. A valid plan must preserve member count and total voting power through conversion.

## F11 The rotation budget permits no replacement in small full sets

**Evidence.** `safe_rotation_cap` in `crates/torus-economics/src/epoch.rs:140` returns `current_set_size / 3`. For four or five validators the budget is one. `apply_rotation_cap` at line 149 counts a replacement as one departure plus one arrival. If that exceeds the budget, it computes `half = max_changes / 2`, so the allowed number of swaps is zero. It removes the proposed arrival and restores the departing validator. `epoch_plan.rs:184–188` applies this arithmetic anew at each planning boundary.

**Counterexample and impact.** With `max_validators=4`, the installed set is A, B, C, D and the stake-ranked proposal is E, A, B, C. One desired replacement produces two counted changes; the budget of one admits zero swaps. D is restored and E removed. Repeating the same unchanged inputs at later epochs produces the same result indefinitely. Five-member full sets behave identically. This can indefinitely prevent an approved, better-ranked candidate from replacing an incumbent. It does not by itself halt block production; governance could change the membership limit, and a non-full set can admit a lone arrival.

**Recommended fix.** Define the cap in explicit replacement units, with separate treatment for growth and shrinkage, and validate the required voting-power overlap. Do not mechanically loosen it based on member count alone when stake weights differ. A policy intended to admit one equal-power replacement in a four-member set should actually admit that transition.

**Regression test.** Exercise full sets of four, five, and six with one replacement and repeated epoch planning. Include only arrivals, only departures, jailed departures, and unequal stake weights. The Python model demonstrates ten successive deferrals for four/five and uses a six-member successful replacement as a control.

## Coverage and unresolved leads

This pass followed proposal/header validation, ancestry and commit indexing, the durable app feed, governance submission/snapshot/voting/finalization, validator registration and rotation, plan construction, and HotStuff update conversion. It did not repeat the first pass's fee, session, storage-error, vote-durability, ADL, and epoch findings.

One consensus lead remains unresolved: `cast_header_vote` at `crates/hotstuff_rs/src/hotstuff/implementation.rs:2735` chooses Prepare versus Generic from `ProposalHeader.has_validator_set_updates`; the body/hash binding does not establish that hint against the application's actual update result. Later `safe_pc` phase checks constrain the outcome. A regression should vary that hint at ordinary and updating blocks and test the resulting QC/recovery behavior. This report does not promote the lead to a proven safety violation or permanent stall.

Cross-VM reads, network queue pressure, full transition fault schedules, disk failures, and live recovery were not exhaustively tested. Four findings in this pass are additional coverage, not a clean bill of health for the remainder of the chain.
